1. IDENTITY (groups a and e)
PROVEN (tables/coreui-memory-classes.txt, scripts/coreui-memclass.py): CoreUI's _gThemeMemoryPerformanceClasses in 24A446's
shared cache, 0x1e1555ca0, entries {value, constant, name}:
   0 kCoreThemeMemoryClassLow 512MB | 1 1GB | 2 2GB | 3 4GB | 4 3GB | 6 6GB | 8 8GB | 9 9GB | 12 12GB | 16 16GB | 15 1.5GB
The value is an enumeration, not a size: 3 is the 4 GB class and 4 the 3 GB one; 5, 7, 10, 11, 13, 14 do not exist.
What is PROVEN here is the table. That the device tree's number IS this enumeration's value rests on take19's reading of
the path (identity-evidence.txt, item 6: ArtworkTraits -> DevicePerformanceMemoryClass, a number -> CoreUI
__deviceMemoryClass, integerValue, absent = 0 = Low; no other reader), not re-read now; Apple's own tree agrees with it
(12 on the 12 GB iPhone18,2). If CoreUI mapped the number some other way, only the Images of 5632 MiB and less would be
off (they write 3 where take19 wrote 4); 6144 and more write 6 either way.
So take19's formula (whole GiB of the VM's RAM) was wrong three ways: 4352/4608 MiB wrote 4 = CoreUI's 3 GB class, 5 GiB
would write 5 and 7 GiB 7, which CoreUI does not hold (what it does with an unknown value: NOT VERIFIED).
take23's formula (runtime/loader, xl_machine_memory_class_of): the largest class whose size the VM's RAM holds, at most Apple's:
   RAM MiB   1024  1536  2048  3072  4096-6143  6144-8191  8192
   value        1    15     2     4          3          6     8
The owner's sizes: 4352, 4608, 5120, 5632 -> 3 ("4GB"); 6144, 6656, 7168 -> 6 ("6GB"). There is no 5 GB class: a 5 GiB VM is
CoreUI's 4 GB class. What the class changes: asset catalogs' memory-class renditions (INFERRED, as in take19).
Group a as the owner chose it: new loader options --identity-omit NAMES and --display-gamut sRGB|P3.
PROVEN on the PC (tables/identity-adt-diff-take22-4608-vs-take23-5120.txt): against take22's tree the new one adds exactly
device-perf-memory-class 0x3, artwork-dynamic-displaymode "0", artwork-device-idiom "phone", artwork-device-subtype 0xb34
(2868), artwork-display-gamut "sRGB"; no graphics-featureset key; everything else that differs is the RAM's size and the
addresses that follow it. Loader tests: 942 + 494 checks pass.
RISK kept from take19's evidence: artwork-device-subtype 2868 selects the safe-area row of a 440x956 pt panel (top inset 62,
bottom 34) on our 360x780 pt screen: the status bar and the home bar may sit lower/higher than now. The -plain Image is the way back.

2. THE NEW BOOT ORDER
What the code and the logs say (PROVEN):
- "2560 pages": gh_hugepage_reserve.c gh_init: pool_size_max = (RAM - min(RAM/2, system_reserve_mb)) / 2 MiB, system_reserve_mb
  6144 by default and settable only at insmod (0400). With the S25 Ultra's MemTotal (11,084 MiB) the table holds about 2770
  pages (5.4 GiB); no pool_want gets past it. oneios-tool's insmod line never passed the parameter, and its --want stopped at 3072.
- The top-up after the stop is NOT new: vm-boot --takeover has topped the pool up after One UI's stop since take18
  (takeover_script: "pool: N pages free, M wanted: topped up while One UI is down"). What refused larger Images was
  vmprobe::check_mem BEFORE the stop: VM + 1024 MiB against MemAvailable + pool, with One UI still up (1.4-2.3 GiB available),
  and its MAX_MEM of 6144. The loader refused RAM above 6144 MiB too.
- What is there after the stop (run.txt, 6 sessions take19-take22b): MemAvailable 3970-4767 MiB BESIDE a pool that already
  held the VM (2160 pages for 4352 MiB; with a pool grown to 2560 pages, 400 pages = 800 MiB sat unused in it).
  So about 9.1-9.8 GiB are to share between the VM and Android once One UI is down.
- What Android needs while the VM runs: NOT MEASURED in any session (no file logs host memory after the start). The GPU's
  blobs are guest pages (shared RAM, drm-host-mb=8), so the host side is crosvm, the renderer and the kernel: INFERRED small.
take23 (oneios-tool, crates/oneios-phone/src/take23.rs; 103 unit tests pass, the same 1 tmpfs failure as before):
- hugepool load --system-reserve-mb N (2048-6144): the module's table. 3072 gives about 4000 pages (8 GiB). --want up to 4096.
- vm-boot --fill-after-stop: the memory rule is asked on the phone AFTER the stop and the cache drop (run.txt `pick:` lines).
- vm-boot --image-larger FILE (repeatable): the candidates are pushed as kernel-RAM.img; the largest is taken for which
  (a) the module's table holds its pages, (b) MemAvailable - 2 MiB x (pages the pool still lacks) - 32 >= --host-keep-mib
  (1024), (c) the file's SHA-256 is the pushed one, (d) the pool's pages come within 120 s (acquire mode 3; the module took
  1536 pages in 20 s and 1961 in 31 s in earlier grows: docs/native.md). pool_size_max and system_reserve_mb are parameters
  of the .ko the owner loads (strings of out/android15-6.6.ko); an unreadable pool_size_max skips (a). A candidate that
  fails gives way to the next; none: the session ends before crosvm starts and One UI comes back (the existing trap).
  The takeover's failsafe and the PC's wait are 200 s longer for each candidate (the choice runs before crosvm starts).
  Pages the pool holds beyond the chosen Image's go back to Android (a lower pool_want makes the module shrink).
- The script's four RAM-bound values (pool pages, crosvm --mem, the GPU arena's address, the kernel file) are read from the
  choice; the tool refuses to boot when it does not find each of them in the script it made (no half-changed script).
- hostmem.txt: MemAvailable, MemFree, pool pages, crosvm's VmRSS every 5 s (the missing measurement).
- The loader takes --ram-mib up to 8192 (crosvm places its PCI window after the RAM: take22b's fdt has it at 0x190800000 for
  4352 MiB; nothing of the layout is at a fixed address above the RAM).
Checked on the PC: the choice's shell run under dash against a fake pool in five situations (pool full, table too small,
pages not coming, nothing fits): it picks, falls back and refuses as written. NOT TESTED on the phone; no Image above 4608 MiB
has ever booted (the QEMU bench stops at 3072 MiB). Expected from the numbers above: 7168 MiB leaves Android about 1.9 GiB;
8192 would leave under 1 GiB and is not built.
No flashing, no partition, nothing outside /data/local/tmp and the module's own sysfs files is written.

  before), with mediaplaybackd at 209 % and 277 % of a CPU and backboardd at 106-109 % (oneios-cpu, all 530 of 600 %).

- the copy (our CPU scaler): "1200 copies ... average 12864 us, failed 0", synchronous on Core Animation's render thread
  (ScreenRecord.inc transformSurface; "events 0/0": no shared event is passed, so none of it is an event wait). At 34 a
  second that is 44 % of the render thread; it alone would cap the screen near 80 a second while recording.
- Why Core Animation's own GPU copy fails ("926 of 927", take18, before the scaler existed): NOT DETERMINED. With

  way: with the encoder at 2.4 CPUs for 34 frames a second no scaler, GPU or CPU, gets the file past ~35.
What reaches 120 (not in take23, a project): the hardware encoder. iOS 27's VideoToolbox has a paravirtualized encoder
(ParavirtualizedVideoEncoder_*), Droid-VM's crosvm has --virtio-media kind=encoder (AMediaCodec), our kernel's codec archive

(1) one session with the decoder Image, (2) the encoder's guest side, (3) ReplayKit's 60 asked up to 120 (replayd), (4) the copy
off the render thread or on the GPU.

7. CACHES: WHAT EXISTS, WHAT WAS ADDED, WHAT COULD BE
Today (PROVEN, take21's section 9 and take22b):
- the translator's disk cache, per process: <Caches>/<process>/m2v/v1 (backboardd: 2460 entries / 16.5 MiB written in take21,
  476 read again and 331 new in take21b). Persists; read at use.
- the base's warm pass: pipeline-records-<process>.bin (backboardd's 1.5 MB), remade in the background at each start; it
  warms the translator, it does not hand Core Animation a ready state.
- OneiOS 0009 (oneios-prewarm=1): kept render pipeline states + prewarm2.log + the boot pass: 572 of 581 ready in take22b (5c).
- the prime list (--prime-list, prime.log): QuartzCore's default.metallib: "kernels=54 renders=39 made=60 ... refused=33
  ... ms=17174"; CoreImage.metallib "made=0 refused=75".
- per process only: backboardd's sandbox denies org.unixnu.metal (take21 section 8); a process without that rule
  (Settings) does use the shared folder.
- dead weight: ~1.2 GiB (58,798 files) of three older plug-in builds' entries in backboardd's folder: disk, not RAM.
Added in take23 (safe, with a switch): 0011's archive asks = Core Animation's own list of the system's pipelines compiled
before they are drawn, including never-seen ones; recorded in prewarm2.log, so the next boot's pass has them too.
The Images carry oneios-prewarm-max=2048 (1024 before): take22b already kept about 600 states and how many the archive
asks add is not known yet; the line's "N not queued" says if the limit binds.
The memory of the extra kept states is not measured (a state is the driver's pipeline object, which exists anyway once used).
Could be cached, NOT done (each with its reason):
- every possible Core Animation pipeline: they are combinations of constants made at run time; the archive asks are the
  list iOS itself gives. If orch/diag logs show asks that are never answered, the next step is the limit oneios-prewarm-max
  (1024 states; the line "N not queued (the 1024 kept states' limit)" says when it binds).
- pipeline packs (NativeAdrenoMetal.m2vpack importer, unwired): a pack made on one phone and shipped in the addon would warm
  a FRESH Data store; the owner's store is already warm, so it gains nothing for him now. Wiring it = plug-in work + a pack
  format tied to the driver build; not safe to ship blind.
- the shared folder for backboardd: needs a sandbox exception in the kernel (a kernel build); gain: a pipeline compiled by
  one process is not compiled by the next. Not in take23 (the kernel is take22's).
- Core Image kernels (iconservicesagent: "binary archive refused ... InvalidFile" 15 times in take22b; killed at its 15 MiB

8. HEAT AND CLOCKS (take22b; tables/t8-take22b.txt, scripts/t8.py; take22's numbers in tables/t8-take22.txt agree)
Sensors (757 samples): CPU sensors max 104.3 / 106.5 C, mean 79.4 / 77.6; GPU 99.2 max; board 57.6; battery 44.1.
A CPU sensor at 100 C or more in 21.5 % of the samples, 103 C or more in 15.7 %.
8.1 Who holds the clocks high (PROVEN)
- The GPU: KGSL's own time in each clock over the session (perf.txt, trans_stat end - start, 981 s): 1200 MHz 66.8 %,
  222 MHz 12.8 %, 607 MHz 9.2 % (the touch boost's floor), 160 MHz 5 %, 1100 MHz 5 %. Its load in the samples at 967-1200 MHz
  (510): mean 19.9 % busy, median 20, p90 38; over 55 % busy in 2.4 % of them.
  Why 1200: not the governor. take18's renderer keeps, by design, backboardd's GPU context at "the highest level" (KGSL
  power constraint) whenever it submits 60 times a second or more, and raises the six vCPU threads' uclamp.min to 1024
  (drm2kgsl_renderer.c kgsl_constraint_update / kgsl_vcpu_animating; crosvm.log: "context 12: power constraint at the highest
  level", "vCPU uclamp.min 1024: 6 of 6 threads (ok)"). vm-boot's default --gpu-host-floors deadline keeps both.
  They were put there in take18/19 against stutter (the governor reads a guest compositor's short bursts as idle).
- The CPUs: in the 62 "use" rounds (guest 148 % of 600) the small cluster's sampled clock is 2.3-3.0 GHz in 67 % of the
  samples and 3.0-3.53 in 14 %; the prime cluster 3.0-4.47 GHz in 40 %. In the 14 heavy rounds (>= 300 %): 3.0-3.53 in 92 %,
  4.0-4.47 in 73 %, the sensors at 100 C. Idle rounds (still screen, 11 %): 1.89 / 1.06 GHz, 60.5 C.
8.2 How much above what the frames need
- GPU frames (59,109; guest submit to host done, at the clocks above): under 4 ms 86 %, 4-8 ms 11.5 %, over the 8.33 ms
  budget 2.6 %. Scaling each 967-1200 MHz sample's load to a lower clock (worst case: all of a frame's time scales with the
  clock, which the host's CPU part does not): at 607 MHz the same work would be over 100 % busy in 3.7 % of those samples and
  over 70 % in 12.2 %; at 734 MHz 2.0 % / 5.7 %; at 832 MHz 2.0 % / 4.1 %; at 967 MHz 0.2 % / 2.2 %.
  So for ~96 % of the time the GPU spends at its top clock, 607 MHz would do the same frames inside the budget.
- CPUs, the host's view (perf.txt thread tables, a minute each; clock = mean of the samples; need = clock x busiest vCPU
  thread / 0.80): time-weighted the small cluster ran at 2.64 GHz where the busiest thread's minute needed 1.52; the prime
  cluster at 2.47 where 1.03 was needed. Minute means hide bursts: this is a floor of the waste's estimate, not a clock to set.
- Which clocks are high while the frames already meet 120 Hz: the 28 moving rounds with 95 % or more of their swaps on time
  ran the GPU at 967-1200 MHz in 82 % of their samples (21 % busy), the prime cluster at 3.0 GHz or more in 49 %, the small
  one at 2.71 GHz on average; the 43 other moving rounds: 80 %, 50 %, 2.80 GHz. The clocks are the same whether the frames
  come or not: they follow "the compositor animates" (the floors), not the frames.
- Swaps while moving (71 rounds): one refresh apart or less 91.2 %, two 6.3 %, more 2.6 %; surfaceflinger's own
  present-to-present: 8 ms 47,862, 16 ms 2,706, 24 ms 717. The misses are not where the clocks are low: the heavy rounds run
  every clock at its top and miss most (CPU-bound: the encoder, app launches).
8.3 Idle against busy; the polling
- Still screen: 10 swaps a second (the poster/clock), each 7.7-8.0 ms of GPU at 222 MHz, backboardd 8-9 % of a CPU, the
  whole guest 11-21 % of 600; the vCPU threads 0-3 % each on the host. Idle is cheap.
- Busy: the host's vCPU threads run more than the guest accounts: e.g. 03:52:06-03:53:07 the guest used 79 % of a CPU, the
  six vCPU threads 169 %; 03:57:10-03:58:10: 114 % against 196 %. The gap (45-90 % of a CPU) is the halt poll (up to 200 us
  a wake-up, gunyah_halt_poll) plus guest time the per-process table does not hold (interrupts, idle exits): NOT separable
  from these logs (the module's parameters and the threads' user/system split are not logged).
- The GPU fence polling inside the guest: oneios-gpu-waits: 27,209 "looks" a round on average (92,710 at most), 8.4 looks a
  wait, spin 300 us, interrupts 0 ("intid 0 (off)"). backboardd's oneios-cpu-why in a menu round: 264,695 "u" and 126,055
  "x" events in 10 s for 675 frames. The -gpuirq Image (the GPU's interrupt instead of the looks) is the A/B for this share;
  it has never run. Its expected gain: the CPU the render server and v_gpu (3-7 % of a host CPU) spend looking.
8.4 Power, and the charger
- The battery's current against the guest's CPU, per round: power out of the battery = -1.56 W + 0.025 W x CPU% (of 600),
  r2 0.77, n 90: about 2.5 W for each fully busy vCPU. Idle rounds: 2.5 W INTO the battery; 150-300 %: 4.95 W out; >= 300 %:
  8.0 W out (11.7 at most), with the charger connected the whole session (usb online 1 in 757 of 757 samples).
- "usb online 1" is most likely the adb cable to the PC, which vm-boot needs: a PC port gives 2.5-4.5 W (0.5-0.9 A at 5 V).
  That fits the idle rounds (2.5 W into the battery with the screen on). So the "charger" here is a small one: under load
  the phone drew 8 W MORE than it gave. INFERRED, not read: the port's own current is not readable on this phone (usb
  current/voltage: no file).
- What the logs cannot separate: the SoC's absolute power and the share of the heat that is the charge (battery 44.1 C,
  board 57.6 C). If Samsung cuts the charge when hot, the 0.025 W per % above is too high by that much. Ten minutes with
  the cable out give both: the battery's current is then the whole system's power. The session survives that: boot.sh
  runs on the phone, detached; the PC's loop ignores failed adb calls until its time is up; the cable must be back
  before the stop so that the files can be pulled. scripts/t8.py prints the battery's power per value of the usb column.
8.5 The compromise shipped: vm-boot --orchestrator (take23.rs ORCH; opt-in by flag = the opt-out is to leave it out)
- GPU: the cap (Samsung's gpu_max_clock, the file the takeover already lifts; the renderer's "highest level" is then that
  cap) starts at 607 MHz and follows the GPU's load 4 times a second: a step up at 55 % busy (two at 70 %), a step down
  after 2 s at 30 % or less; steps 607/660/734/832/900/967/1050/1100/1200. The clocks under the cap stay the governor's
  and the touch boost's.
- vCPUs: the clamp the renderer gives the vCPU threads at each animation (its own file, /data/local/tmp/drm2kgsl-vcpu-uclamp)
  starts at 1024 and follows the GUEST's frames: every 10 s the kernel's oneios-display-pace line (swaps on time while
  moving); 92 % or more on time: a step (128) down, to 512 at the least; under 85 %: two steps up and the GPU's cap to its top
  for 10 s. Between: kept. So it finds the lowest clamp that keeps the frames, and gives back at once when they slip.
- Heat: a CPU sensor at 98 C: the clamp and the cap go a step down every 2 s whatever the frames say.
- Numbers changeable per run: --orchestrator-set gpu-floor=, gpu-up=, gpu-down=, gpu-hold=, clamp-floor=, clamp-step=,
  good=, bad=, hot=.
- At the end the cap is the lifted one and the clamp file is gone; caps_restore puts Samsung's cap back as before.
Checked on the PC: the loop run under dash against fake sysfs files (steps up at 60/80 %, down after 8 looks, a good and a
bad round, a hot sensor, the end). NOT TESTED on the phone. Not claimed: a number of degrees. The GPU's rail power is not
in any log; the estimate above is time at clocks, not watts.
